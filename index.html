About

Law, public policy, human rights and institutional development.

Gerson Ruiz brings more than 30 years of experience across law, human rights, public policy, advocacy, and institutional development in Latin America, Europe, and the United States.

A lawyer, researcher, and social entrepreneur, his work spans law, governance, administration, development, artificial intelligence, and institutional design. His professional experience includes public service in Peru, international work in Geneva, immigration and human-rights advocacy in the United States, and the development of nonprofit initiatives serving immigrant communities.

He studied law and political science at the University of Lima and pursued graduate and professional studies at George Washington University, American University, the University of the District of Columbia, Oxford University, and Duke University.

Gerson is the founder and Executive Director of Immigration Law Human Rights (ILHR). His current research examines how human contribution, participation, and governance can be preserved as artificial intelligence becomes increasingly autonomous.

Research

Can effective human governance of advanced AI be sustained as humans become progressively less economically necessary to the systems they govern?

Acknowledge Economy (AE)

An experimental institutional and corporate-governance model testing whether validated human contribution can remain connected to economic and institutional participation as automation reduces dependence on continuing human labor and knowledge.

AJ-OS

An experimental operating system for identifying, preserving, validating, and attributing contributions and connecting them to measurable organizational impact.

Contribution → Participation → Governance → Control
Contribution → Event → Validation → Impact
